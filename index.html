03 — Florida, by the reality

Why Florida roofs wear out sooner

Hurricanes, driving rain, intense UV and year-round humidity punish Florida roofs like nowhere else in the country. That’s why small problems here become expensive ones fast.

Wind & storms

Hurricane-season winds lift shingles, break seals and drive rain under the roof surface — damage that often starts invisibly from the ground.

Sun & heat

Relentless UV and attic heat bake shingles year after year, drying them out, curling edges and shedding the protective granules.

Rain & humidity

Heavy daily rains find every weak point — flashing, valleys, pipe boots — and Florida humidity keeps small leaks wet instead of letting them dry.

07 — Florida law

Florida law, in plain English

In Florida, roofing work must be performed by — or under — a licensed roofing contractor. That’s exactly how Roof Crews Direct is structured:

  • We are a marketing and lead-generation company. Roof Crews Direct is not a roofing contractor and does not perform roofing work.
  • Your contract is with the licensed contractor who performs the work — their license, their permits, their warranty obligations.
  • The contractor’s identity is confirmed to you before you sign anything — at the proposal stage, you know exactly who will be on your roof.
  • Our markup is disclosed as part of the price you agree to — it is not a hidden fee added later.

This section is a plain-English summary of how the work is structured, not legal advice.

10 — Transparent pricing

How our pricing works — the disclosed markup

Most roofing quotes are one opaque number. Ours is an equation we show you:

Contractor’s real cost  +  our disclosed markup  =  your price

  • The licensed contractor sets their cost for your job — labor, materials, dumpster, permits.
  • We add our markup on top, and the contractor knows it — that’s what makes it clean and legal. It is not a referral fee taken in secret from a price that was padded to hide it.
  • You see the structure in your proposal, and the contractor collects the contract price and pays us our share.

Compare us

Before you pay for a big name, compare what you actually get

Ask every bidder — including us — the same six questions. They are the criteria we build every proposal around, and the fastest way to make a decision you won’t regret.

1 · Analysis of your situation

“Do they understand what I want AND what my roof needs — and help me buy, rather than convince me?”

Look for roof-specific documentation: measured quantities, documented findings, what they mean for your home, and choices tied to your needs — not a one-size pitch.

2 · An affordable solution

“Did their recommendations align with my needs, my concerns and my budget?”

Look for a solution built from your situation: the manufacturers and design details explained, the complete price in the open, and a company you can trust. Affordable means right-sized — not stripped down.

3 · Quality installation

“Is the assigned crew experienced and qualified — and how is the installation checked?”

Look for relevant credentials, Florida experience, the responsible business’s license and insurance, documented installation checks, and references you can actually call.

4 · Weather protection

“Is it designed for extreme weather — or only for minimum requirements?”

Look for deck protection, specified fasteners, new flashing where required, starter strips at eaves and rakes, and installation matched to the selected system — built for Florida storms, not just for minimums.

5 · Ventilation to code

“Do they understand why ventilation matters — and the building code behind it?”

A serious bidder explains it before you ask. Ventilation protects your family’s health — trapped attic moisture feeds mold — keeps attic heat from driving up your cooling costs, and protects your warranty, because manufacturers can exclude damage caused by inadequate ventilation. GAF’s training materials estimate that around 90% of North American homes are inadequately ventilated; a proper proposal checks yours and documents the fix to code.

6 · Superior value

“Do I trust the company, the solution, the installation, the protection and the ventilation?”

Look for clarity: you understand what is included, why it is included, what your options are, and what the full investment is — with no surprises after you sign.

Compare your experience. Compare your proposal. Compare your quote.
Then ask: who do you trust — the big-name contractor who subcontracts the work, or the people who actually do the work, direct?

14 — Warranty

Warranty — in the contractor’s name, in writing

Your work is covered by the licensed contractor’s warranty: their workmanship warranty on the installation, together with the manufacturer’s warranty on the materials installed — both as provided by the contractor and the manufacturer, and both delivered to you in writing at contracting.

The exact workmanship term and the manufacturer coverage for the system you select are confirmed in writing by the responsible roofing business in your final proposal. When you compare bids, compare the term, the coverage and the exclusions — and who actually responds if you ever need it — not just a headline number of years.

Roof Crews Direct coordinates your project, but warranties on roofing work belong to — and are honored by — the licensed contractor who performs it. In your final proposal, the contractor is named, so you know exactly whose warranty you hold before you sign.
